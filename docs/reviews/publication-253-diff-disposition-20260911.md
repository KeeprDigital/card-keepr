## Recovery and maintenance judgment

The hardest part of batching is safe interruption, not performing several PUTs
at once. If three writes finish and one response is lost, retry must discover
the persisted object, verify its bytes and settle only the actual observed
writer. A cursor cannot certify a partial batch. Current production code and
the new regressions explicitly preserve this ownership chain; the
[staging admission/deletion triggers](../../migrations/0024_evidence_cleanup.sql)
remain in force. That complexity serves a real requirement. Increasing one
phase from six to eleven units merely changes how much of that work shares a
checkpoint and is a separate, weaker proposition.

The [callback guard](../../apps/ingestion/src/reconciliation-resource-budget.ts)
counts one binding `batch` invocation as one call, even when the transaction
contains many SQL statements. The
[store](../../src/catalogue/shared/catalogue-store-repository.ts) separately
limits expanded atomic batches to 900 statements. Consequently, fewer measured
calls can mean fewer round trips without fewer SQL row scans, writes or retained
bytes. No part of this diff establishes provider billing, production CPU/RAM,
or usable 5/50 GiB database capacity.

The ledger's 99 projection / 97 private-export call counts are analytical
worst-path estimates, not measurements of all collision and failure branches.
They leave one and three calls below the unchanged guard. The guard is useful
failure containment, but repeatedly hitting it can replay a whole uncommitted
batch and exhaust retries. Promotion needs a small real-D1/R2 boundary case
covering registered-but-missing objects, conditional-write collisions,
ambiguous writer settlement and final-fence handling together. Merely adding
more stopwatch runs would not supply that proof. Byte bounds likewise are
design limits on retained/bound payloads, not reliable process-memory readings.

The next production change should be chosen after the isolated component-layout
comparison. Existing one-record format assertions remain authoritative during
that comparison. A format change needs explicit producer, consumer, verification
and recovery contract updates; none of the batching above authorizes it. Keep
the full 1,001-Product workload and its actual backup/restore/export assertions.
The original 120-second failures remain failures under their original
acceptance, regardless of routine CI passes or a proposed future hang budget.

Until that decision, preserve the working tree and evidence; select hunks into
reviewable groups rather than making a bulk rollback. The smallest clearly
unsupported knob to remove is the eleven-unit exception. The highest-value
retained group is bounded repeated-work elimination with the new replay and
ownership tests. The late set-based/registry/fact group remains a promising,
unpromoted experiment, not an established requirement.
